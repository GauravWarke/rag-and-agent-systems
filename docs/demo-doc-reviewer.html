Multimodal Document Reviewer

OCR is cheap and sometimes wrong. A vision model is accurate and expensive. A human is the most accurate and by far the most expensive. Move the two thresholds and watch cost trade against errors — the whole engineering problem is choosing where they sit.

What's real: the cascade routing, cost model and error projection all compute live from the confidence figures below. The per-field OCR and vision confidences are a fixed sample set standing in for actual model output — running real OCR in a browser isn't possible, and inventing confidences per keystroke would be worse than using a stated sample. Wrong fields is an expectation, not a count: each accepted field contributes its own (1 − confidence). Human review is modelled at 99.5% accurate — better than either model, but not free and not perfect.

Reading the trade-off

Push escalate to vision to 1.00 and every field goes to the vision model. Errors drop and cost climbs roughly tenfold. Drop it to 0.00 and you're running OCR alone — nearly free, and on a faxed document about one field in four comes out wrong.

Neither extreme is the answer, and the interesting part is that the cascade beats both on the combined measure while only escalating the fields that need it. That's the whole idea: spend the expensive capability where confidence says it's warranted, not uniformly.

The second threshold matters more than it looks. Human review is roughly fifty times the cost of a vision call. Raising escalate to human a few points can double your operating cost while removing a fraction of a percent of errors. Try 0.85 versus 0.95 on the faxed profile and watch what happens to the monthly figure.

Worth noticing: signature_block is the weakest field on every profile. Some fields are hard for reasons no threshold fixes — the right response there is a schema that marks the field optional, or a different capture process, not a more expensive model.
